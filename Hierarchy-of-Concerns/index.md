### Landings Park

**The ambulance waited** with its engine off,
in competition for the shade.
Between noon and six,
the best asphalt lot in town is harsh awhile and same anybody,
they have to stop somewhere.

They don’t just stop existing between calls.
And they _certainly_ don’t roll home and back every time.
Have you seen the price of gas?
This is the war economy.

That’s what the broadsheets say,
news from elsewhere
filtered through eight hundred lenses,
and at least by the liar who said.

Across from them (directly across)
was the back of a white work-in-progress VW Minivan
that had just failed its registration-required tests.
His current car was down the road,
and so he refused the assortment of snacks I could spare.

We didn’t have jumper cables.
I was willing, but unable.
I was willing in particular because I’d been asked the same thing
once already that morning but failed to help for the same reason.
Second Chances are worth collecting when they appear.
Rarely will you find a better ground score.

He walked off to buy cables,
and he was well enough for things that he got that done
without long elaboration of how.
He returned and I roused from my sunny spot
to go to the one aimed at him.

Nothing.

My muffler sounds like a V6, someone said yesterday.
It’s actually a stuck cylinder four that fires third-ish.
It sounds meaner. Sicker, anyway.
I’m mostly worried someone can touch pincher that blow me up.
Like a bomb scene, when what you want doesn’t happen,
there is a shallow pool of reasons why,
and none of them are worth inspecting as urgently
as the unidentified consequences rush near.

It’s not taking the charge.

Bummer, I’m saying.
Even once you’re socially square with someone
for renewing moments of shared attention,
commiseration for a failed jumpstart is a stretch.
My car is like, “Turn Me Offffghugh-hughg-ghugh-hughg.”

But we spoke. 
I would’ve recorded everything
but you only know when to start when it got good.
I asked him.

Yeah.
